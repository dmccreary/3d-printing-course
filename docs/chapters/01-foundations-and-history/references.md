# References: Foundations and History of Additive Manufacturing

1. [3D printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_printing) - Wikipedia - Comprehensive history of additive manufacturing from the 1980s through the desktop revolution, covering key inventors, patent milestones, and the emergence of the maker movement.

2. [Chuck Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Hull) - Wikipedia - Biography of the inventor of stereolithography and co-founder of 3D Systems, who filed the first AM patent in 1986 and launched the commercial additive manufacturing industry.

3. [Fused deposition modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fused_deposition_modeling) - Wikipedia - History and technical overview of FDM, including Scott Crump's 1989 patent, Stratasys' commercialization, and the explosion of desktop printers after patent expiration in 2009.

4. Additive Manufacturing Technologies (3rd ed.) — Ian Gibson, David Rosen, Brent Stucker, Mahyar Khorasani — Springer, 2021 — Chapters 1–2 trace the full arc of AM history from early stereolithography through modern desktop systems with technical depth and industry context.

5. Fabricated: The New World of 3D Printing — Hod Lipson and Melba Kurman — Wiley, 2013 — Accessible narrative history aimed at a broad audience, covering key milestones, visionary thinkers, and the social implications of personal fabrication.

6. [RepRap](https://reprap.org/wiki/RepRap) - RepRap Project - The open-source RepRap documentation explaining how the expiration of Stratasys' FDM patents sparked the self-replicating desktop printer movement and global maker culture.

7. [What is FDM 3D printing?](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/what-is-fdm-3d-printing/) - Hubs (Protolabs Network) - Introduction to FDM technology covering how the process works, materials, and applications — providing present-day context for the history covered in this chapter.

8. [Project Lead The Way](https://pltw.org) - PLTW - The national STEM education organization whose engineering curriculum integrates additive manufacturing and connects this course's content to workforce-aligned pathways.

9. [Printables](https://www.printables.com) - Prusa Research - Community platform hosting millions of printable designs, illustrating how open-source culture and patent expiration transformed 3D printing from an industrial tool into a global maker movement.

10. [Selecting the right 3D printing process](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/selecting-right-3d-printing-process/) - Hubs (Protolabs Network) - Overview of four major AM processes (FDM, SLA, SLS, MJF) that traces how each technology emerged from the historical foundations covered in this chapter.
